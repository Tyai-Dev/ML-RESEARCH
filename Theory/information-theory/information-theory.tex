\noindent
The quantities behind every ``cross-entropy loss'' line of ML code, and
the identity that ties this whole repository together: minimizing NLL is
minimizing a KL divergence. \texttt{information\_theory.py} verifies each
claim numerically.

\section{Entropy}

\begin{definition}
For a distribution $p$ on a finite alphabet,
\begin{equation}
  H(p) \;=\; -\sum_x p(x)\log p(x)
  \qquad (0\log 0 := 0).
  \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

$H$ measures the uncertainty of a draw --- equivalently (source coding
theorem) the optimal average code length, in nats for $\log_e$, bits for
$\log_2$. Extremes: $H = 0$ iff $p$ is deterministic; on $k$ symbols $H$
is maximized by the uniform distribution at $\log k$. The Bernoulli curve
$H(p) = -p\log p - (1-p)\log(1-p)$ peaks at $p=\tfrac12$ (script, left
panel). Among densities on $\mathbb{R}$ with fixed variance, the Gaussian
maximizes (differential) entropy --- one reason it is the default noise
model.

\section{KL divergence}

\begin{definition}
For $p \ll q$ (i.e.\ $q(x)=0 \Rightarrow p(x)=0$),
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q) \;=\; \sum_x p(x) \log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} .
  \label{eq:kl}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The expected excess code length for encoding $p$-data with a code built
for $q$ --- the natural ``how wrong is model $q$ about reality $p$.''

\begin{theorem}[Gibbs' inequality]
$\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q) \ge 0$, with equality iff $p = q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $\log t \le t - 1$ (equality iff $t=1$), for $x$ with $p(x)>0$:
$-\mathrm{KL} = \sum_x p(x)\log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)} \le
\sum_x p(x)\bigl(\frac{q(x)}{p(x)} - 1\bigr) = \sum_x q(x) - 1 \le 0$,
with equality iff $q(x)=p(x)$ everywhere $p$ charges.
\end{proof}

Not a metric: asymmetric, no triangle inequality. The direction matters
--- $\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)$ punishes $q$ for assigning tiny mass where $p$
lives (mode-covering); $\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)$ tolerates dropping modes.

\section{Cross-entropy, and the identity behind the loss}

\begin{definition}
$H(p, q) = -\sum_x p(x)\log q(x)$.
\end{definition}

Adding and subtracting $H(p)$ gives the decomposition
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;H(p, q) \;=\; H(p) \;+\; \mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q).\;}
  \label{eq:cedecomp}
\end{equation}
Since $H(p)$ does not involve $q$: \emph{minimizing cross-entropy over the
model $q$ is minimizing the KL divergence to it}, and $H(p)$ is the
irreducible floor of the loss.

\section{Maximum likelihood is KL minimization}

Let $\hat p$ be the \emph{empirical distribution} of a sample
$x_1,\dots,x_n$ (mass $\tfrac1n$ at each observation). For any model
$q_\theta$, the average NLL rewrites as
\begin{equation}
  \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n -\log q_\theta(x_i)
  \;=\; -\sum_x \hat p(x) \log q_\theta(x)
  \;=\; H(\hat p, q_\theta)
  \;\overset{\eqref{eq:cedecomp}}{=}\;
  H(\hat p) + \mathrm{KL}(\hat p \,\|\, q_\theta) .
  \label{eq:mlekl}
\end{equation}
The $\theta$-dependence sits entirely in the KL term, hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\arg\min_\theta \mathrm{NLL}(\theta)
  \;=\; \arg\min_\theta \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\hat p \,\|\, q_\theta\bigr):\;}
  \label{eq:mleiskl}
\end{equation}
maximum likelihood fits the model to the data's empirical distribution in
the KL sense. Every MLE in this repository --- Bernoulli, Gaussian, least
squares --- is an instance; the script overlays $\mathrm{NLL}(q)$ and
$H(\hat p) + \mathrm{KL}(\hat p\|q)$ for Bernoulli and the curves
coincide to $10^{-12}$, with the minimum at $\bar x$ sitting on the
entropy floor. (This also explains why the loss never reaches zero: the
floor is $H(\hat p)$, the data's own uncertainty.)

\section{Mutual information}

\begin{definition}
$I(X;Y) = \mathrm{KL}\bigl(p_{XY} \,\|\, p_X p_Y\bigr)
= H(X) - H(X\mid Y) = H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)$.
\end{definition}

The KL distance of the joint from independence --- how many nats one
variable carries about the other; zero iff independent, and symmetric
despite KL's asymmetry. It upper-bounds what any predictor of $Y$ from
$X$ can achieve (Fano), and appears across ML as the objective of
representation learning (InfoNCE, information bottleneck) and the
capacity limit of channels. Worth internalizing next to
\eqref{eq:mleiskl}: entropy is the floor of prediction, mutual
information is the budget of relevance.
