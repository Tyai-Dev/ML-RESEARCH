\section{Bernoulli$(p)$}

$x \in \{0,1\}$, parameter $p \in (0,1)$:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(x) = p^{x}(1-p)^{1-x},
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[x] = p,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Var}(x) = p(1-p).
  \label{eq:bern}
\end{equation}
Variance is maximal at $p=\tfrac12$ and vanishes at the deterministic
endpoints. MLE from $n$ i.i.d.\ samples: $\hat p = m/n$ with
$m=\sum_i x_i$ (derived in \texttt{bernoulli/}); $m$ is sufficient.
Exponential-family form with natural parameter the log-odds
$t = \log\frac{p}{1-p}$ (the logit — the reason the sigmoid keeps
appearing).

\section{Multinoulli / categorical$(p_1,\dots,p_k)$}

One draw among $k$ outcomes, $\sum_j p_j = 1$. With the one-hot encoding
$e_x \in \{0,1\}^k$:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(x=j) = p_j,
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[e_x] = p,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Cov}(e_x)_{ij} =
  \begin{cases}
    p_i(1-p_i) & i=j\\[2pt]
    -\,p_i p_j & i\neq j .
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:multinoulli}
\end{equation}
The negative off-diagonal covariance is structural: outcomes compete ---
one category occurring excludes the others. Over $n$ draws the counts
$(n_1,\dots,n_k)$ are multinomial and sufficient; the MLE (a constrained
problem solved with a Lagrange multiplier, cf.\ the optimization note) is
\begin{equation}
  \hat p_j \;=\; \frac{n_j}{n}.
  \label{eq:multimle}
\end{equation}
Softmax is to the multinoulli what the sigmoid is to the Bernoulli: the
map from unconstrained scores to the probability simplex.

\section{Normal $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$}

$x \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
  f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}
         \exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[x]=\mu,\quad \mathrm{Var}(x)=\sigma^2 .
  \label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
Facts to keep at hand: closed under affine maps
($ax+b \sim \mathcal{N}(a\mu+b,\, a^2\sigma^2)$); sums of independent
normals are normal; $68/95/99.7\%$ of mass within $1/2/3$ standard
deviations; maximizes entropy among densities on $\mathbb{R}$ with fixed
variance (information-theory note); the CLT makes it the universal limit
of standardized sums. MLE: $\hat\mu = \bar x$, $\hat\sigma^2 =
\tfrac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2$ --- biased by design (\texttt{gaussian/});
$(\sum x_i, \sum x_i^2)$ is sufficient.

\section{Multivariate normal $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$}

$x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^d$, covariance
$\Sigma \succ 0$ symmetric:
\begin{equation}
  f(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\,|\Sigma|^{1/2}}
         \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac12 (x-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x-\mu)\Bigr).
  \label{eq:mvn}
\end{equation}
$\mathbb{E}[x]=\mu$, $\mathrm{Cov}(x)=\Sigma$. Level sets are ellipsoids
whose axes are the eigenvectors of $\Sigma$ with half-lengths
$\propto\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (drawn by the script). Closed under affine maps:
$Ax+b \sim \mathcal{N}(A\mu+b,\, A\Sigma A^\top)$; all marginals and
conditionals are again normal --- the property that makes Gaussians the
workhorse of tractable multivariate modeling. For Gaussians (and only
them, among joint normals) uncorrelated $\Rightarrow$ independent. MLE
from $n$ samples:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\mu = \bar x,
  \qquad
  \hat\Sigma = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\bar x)(x_i-\bar x)^\top ,
  \label{eq:mvnmle}
\end{equation}
again the $1/n$ (biased) form.
