\begin{abstract}
The Bernoulli program (\texttt{bernoulli/}) repeated for the 1D normal
distribution: derive the closed-form MLE of $(\mu,\sigma)$, reach the same
optimum by gradient descent and by SGD on the negative log-likelihood, and
verify that PyTorch autograd reproduces the hand-derived gradients to
floating-point precision. New here: a two-dimensional parameter, and the
fact that the MLE of the variance is the \emph{biased} $1/n$ estimator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: from the likelihood to the NLL}

Let $x_1,\dots,x_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$
with density
$f(x) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}
\exp\!\bigl(-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2\bigr)$. By independence,
\begin{equation}
  L(\mu,\sigma)
  \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i)
  \;=\; (2\pi)^{-n/2}\,\sigma^{-n}
        \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\mu)^2\Bigr).
  \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
Taking $\log$ (strictly increasing: same maximizer), dividing by $n>0$ and
negating (neither moves the optimizer — the same three steps as in the
Bernoulli note) gives the average negative log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\mu,\sigma)
  \;=\; \tfrac12\log(2\pi) + \log\sigma
        + \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\cdot\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\mu)^2 .
  \label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
Expanding $(x_i-\mu)^2$ shows the data enters only through
$\sum_i x_i$ and $\sum_i x_i^2$: a two-dimensional sufficient statistic.

\section{Route 1: the theoretical solution}

\begin{proposition}
The unique MLE is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\hat\mu = \xbar, \qquad
  \hat\sigma^2 = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\xbar)^2 . \;}
  \label{eq:mle}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:nll} in $\mu$:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\mu}
  = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\cdot\frac1n\sum_i(\mu - x_i)
  = \frac{\mu-\xbar}{\sigma^2} = 0
  \;\Longrightarrow\; \mu = \xbar
\end{equation*}
(for every $\sigma$, so the two equations decouple). Differentiate in
$\sigma$ at $\mu=\xbar$, writing $v = \tfrac1n\sum_i (x_i-\xbar)^2$:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\sigma}
  = \frac{1}{\sigma} - \frac{v}{\sigma^{3}} = 0
  \;\Longrightarrow\; \sigma^{2} = v .
\end{equation*}
For uniqueness, $\mathcal{L}(\xbar,\sigma)$ has derivative
$(\sigma^2-v)/\sigma^3$: negative for $\sigma^2<v$, positive for
$\sigma^2>v$, so $v$ is the unique minimum in $\sigma$; and for each
$\sigma$ the map $\mu\mapsto\mathcal{L}$ is a strictly convex parabola
minimized at $\xbar$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The $1/n$ is not a mistake.}
$\mathbb{E}[\hat\sigma^2] = \tfrac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$: the MLE of the
variance is \emph{biased}. Bessel's $\tfrac{1}{n-1}$ correction is a
different estimator, chosen to be unbiased; maximum likelihood genuinely
produces $1/n$. (The script prints both.)

\section{Route 2: gradient descent}

$\mu$ is already unconstrained; $\sigma>0$ is not. Exactly as Bernoulli's
$p=\operatorname{sigmoid}(t)$, we evaluate the \emph{same} NLL in an
unconstrained coordinate, $\sigma = e^{s}$:
\begin{equation}
  F(\mu, s) \;=\; \mathcal{L}(\mu, e^{s})
  \;=\; \tfrac12\log(2\pi) + s
        + \frac{1}{2e^{2s}}\cdot\frac1n\sum_i (x_i-\mu)^2 .
  \label{eq:reparam}
\end{equation}
Chain rule ($\partial\sigma/\partial s = e^s$):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial F}{\partial \mu} = \frac{\mu-\xbar}{e^{2s}},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial F}{\partial s}
  = 1 - \frac{1}{e^{2s}}\cdot\frac1n\sum_i (x_i-\mu)^2 .
  \label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}
Setting both to zero reproduces \eqref{eq:mle} — a consistency check on
the derivation. GD iterates the full gradient
$(\mu, s) \leftarrow (\mu, s) - \eta\,\nabla F$ and converges to it
deterministically (both coordinates visible in the middle panel of the
script's figure).

\section{Route 3: stochastic gradient descent}

Replace the full gradient with the one-sample estimate. Per sample $i$,
$F_i(\mu,s) = s + (x_i-\mu)^2/(2e^{2s}) + \mathrm{const}$, so
\begin{equation}
  g_i \;=\; \Bigl(\;\frac{\mu - x_i}{e^{2s}}\;,\;\;
             1 - \frac{(x_i-\mu)^2}{e^{2s}}\;\Bigr),
  \qquad \mathbb{E}_i[g_i] = \nabla F ,
  \label{eq:sgdgrad}
\end{equation}
an unbiased but noisy direction. With a constant step the iterate hovers
in a noise ball around \eqref{eq:mle}; averaging the final pass's iterates
(Polyak--Ruppert) cancels the hovering. Mini-batches would average $g_i$
over a batch — same expectation, variance $\div\,|B|$; engineering, not
new mathematics.

\section{Route 4: SGD with autograd}

The script writes the per-sample loss \emph{verbatim} as
$s + (x_i-\mu)^2/(2e^{2s})$ in torch and lets \texttt{backward()}
differentiate the composition: it must produce exactly
\eqref{eq:sgdgrad}. Driving routes 3 and 4 through the identical sample
schedule, step size, and initialization, the asserted bound
$\max_k \lVert\theta_k^{\text{hand}} - \theta_k^{\text{autograd}}\rVert
< 10^{-10}$ passes — autograd mechanizes the chain rule; it does not
change the mathematics.

\section{Experiment}

With $(\mu,\sigma) = (2, 1.5)$ and $n = 5000$ (seed 7): the closed form
gives $(\hat\mu,\hat\sigma) \approx (2.011, 1.502)$; GD converges to it in
a few hundred steps; SGD's Polyak average lands within a few $10^{-2}$
(the variance coordinate is noisier than the mean's); and the hand and
autograd trajectories coincide exactly. Same story as Bernoulli, one
dimension higher: the likelihood surface decides, the optimizer only
travels.
