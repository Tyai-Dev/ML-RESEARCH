\section{The distribution}

$X \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$: support $\{0,1\}$, pmf
$f(x;p) = p^x (1-p)^{1-x}$ (a case split written as one expression),
$\mathbb{E}[X] = p$, $\mathrm{Var}(X) = p(1-p)$ --- maximal at
$p = \tfrac12$, vanishing at the certain ends. For an i.i.d.\ sample
$x_1..x_n$ with $m = \sum_i x_i$ ones,
\begin{equation}
  L(p) = \prod_i p^{x_i}(1-p)^{1-x_i} = p^m (1-p)^{n-m}:
  \label{eq:lik}
\end{equation}
the data enters only through the count $m$ --- a \emph{sufficient
statistic}. Verified by sampling in \texttt{bernoulli.py} (part 1).

\section{Statistics of the estimator}

The estimator below will be $\phat = m/n = \xbar$; it is a random
variable, judged by its distribution:
\textbf{unbiased} ($\mathbb{E}[\phat] = p$, since
$m \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)$); \textbf{variance}
$p(1-p)/n$, whose square root is the standard error; \textbf{efficient}
--- the Fisher information per sample is $I(p) = 1/(p(1-p))$, so the
Cram\'er--Rao bound $1/(nI(p)) = p(1-p)/n$ is attained with equality
(Theory/statistics); \textbf{MSE} $=$ variance (unbiasedness);
\textbf{consistent} ($\phat \to p$: the LLN); \textbf{asymptotically
normal}, $\sqrt{n}(\phat - p) \to \mathcal{N}(0, p(1-p))$, buying the
$95\%$ interval $\phat \pm 1.96\sqrt{\phat(1-\phat)/n}$.

\section{MLE and MAP}

\paragraph{MLE.} Take logs of \eqref{eq:lik} (monotone, so the argmax
stays): $\ell(p) = m \log p + (n-m)\log(1-p)$. Then
$\ell'(p) = m/p - (n-m)/(1-p) = 0 \Rightarrow m(1-p) = (n-m)p
\Rightarrow m = np$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\phat_{\mathrm{MLE}} = \frac{m}{n} = \xbar\;}
  \label{eq:mle}
\end{equation}
unique ($\ell'' < 0$: strict concavity); at the edge cases $m \in
\{0, n\}$ the supremum sits on the boundary and the formula survives.

\paragraph{MAP: the prior as pseudo-counts.} Give $p$ a
$\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$ prior, $\pi(p) \propto
p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$. The posterior multiplies pmf and prior:
\begin{equation*}
  \pi(p \mid x) \;\propto\; p^{m + \alpha - 1}(1-p)^{n - m + \beta - 1}
  \;=\; \mathrm{Beta}(m + \alpha,\; n - m + \beta)
\end{equation*}
--- the same functional form (Beta is \emph{conjugate} to Bernoulli),
so maximizing it is the MLE computation with shifted counts:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\phat_{\mathrm{MAP}}
  = \frac{m + \alpha - 1}{n + \alpha + \beta - 2}\;}
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Read it as \emph{pseudo-counts}: the prior contributes $\alpha - 1$
imaginary ones and $\beta - 1$ imaginary zeros before any data
arrives. Three consequences, all verified in the script:
$\alpha = \beta = 1$ (flat prior) recovers the MLE exactly;
as $n \to \infty$ the data drowns the prior and
$\phat_{\mathrm{MAP}} \to \phat_{\mathrm{MLE}}$; and at SMALL $n$ the
shrinkage toward the prior mean trades a little bias for a lot of
variance --- measured, MAP beats MLE in MSE on tiny samples.

\begin{remark}[We have been doing MAP all along]
Laplace/add-$\alpha$ smoothing --- used since the bigram model and the
BPE evaluation (Generative/text-to-text) --- \emph{is}
\eqref{eq:map}'s multinoulli analog with a Dirichlet prior: smoothing
is not a hack, it is a prior confessed.
\end{remark}

\section{Solving the MLE with SGD}

The ML translation: normalize and negate $\ell$ (neither moves the
optimizer) into the average NLL
$\mathcal{L}(p) = -\xbar\log p - (1-\xbar)\log(1-p)$, then free the
constrained $p \in (0,1)$ through the sigmoid --- the SAME objective in
a new coordinate, $F(t) = \mathcal{L}(\sigma(t))$. The chain rule
collapses to
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;F'(t) = \sigma(t) - \xbar, \qquad
  g_i(t) = \sigma(t) - x_i\;}
  \label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}
(full and per-sample gradients: residuals). SGD steps
$t \leftarrow t - \eta\, g_i(t)$ one sample at a time: unbiased
($\mathbb{E}_i[g_i] = F'$), cheap, and with constant $\eta$ it never
converges --- it hovers in a noise ball, cured by Polyak--Ruppert
averaging of the final epoch. $F'' = \sigma' > 0$: one basin. By MLE
\emph{invariance}, $\hat p = \sigma(\hat t\,)$ --- the reparameterization
is harmless. (Autograd on
\texttt{binary\_cross\_entropy\_with\_logits} reproduces
\eqref{eq:grad} to machine precision --- checked in the script, the
repo's standard proof by computation.)

\section{Experiment}

\texttt{bernoulli.py}, $p = 0.3$, seed 7: sampling verifies both
moments to $10^{-3}$; the closed form gives $\phat = 0.2978$
($n = 5000$), matching a brute-force argmax and covered by its $95\%$
interval; MAP with $\mathrm{Beta}(3,3)$ at $n = 20$ beats the MLE's
MSE (measured over $20{,}000$ replications) and coincides with it to
$10^{-3}$ by $n = 5000$; the logged SGD loop descends to the noise
ball and the Polyak average lands within $10^{-3}$ of $\xbar$, with
the autograd trajectory identical to the hand loop.
