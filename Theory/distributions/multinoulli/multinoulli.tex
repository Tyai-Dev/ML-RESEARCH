\noindent
One draw, $K$ outcomes: $\Pr(X = j) = p_j$, with $p$ on the simplex
($p_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j p_j = 1$). Bernoulli is the case $K = 2$. In
one-hot coordinates $e_X \in \{0,1\}^K$ the pmf is
$\prod_j p_j^{\,[X = j]}$ and everything becomes linear algebra.

\section{Moments}

Each coordinate of $e_X$ is a bernoulli indicator, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[e_X] = p, \qquad
  \operatorname{Cov}[e_X] = \operatorname{diag}(p) - p\,p^{\!\top}:
  \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
variances $p_j(1 - p_j)$ on the diagonal, and for $i \ne j$
\begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{1}[X{=}i], \mathbf{1}[X{=}j])
  = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}[X{=}i]\mathbf{1}[X{=}j]] - p_i p_j
  = 0 - p_i p_j = -p_i p_j
\end{equation*}
--- \emph{negative}: one category firing forbids the others. The
companion script verifies the entire matrix \eqref{eq:moments} against
$2 \times 10^5$ samples.

\section{Maximum likelihood}

For $n$ i.i.d.\ draws with counts $n_j$, the log-likelihood is
$\ell(p) = \sum_j n_j \log p_j$. Maximizing subject to
$\sum_j p_j = 1$ (Lagrange): $n_j / p_j = \lambda$ for every $j$, and
the constraint forces $\lambda = n$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\hat{p}_j = \frac{n_j}{n}\;}
  \label{eq:mle}
\end{equation}
--- count and normalize; bernoulli's $m/n$, one level up. Optimality
the information-theoretic way: for any challenger $q$,
$\mathrm{NLL}(q) - \mathrm{NLL}(\hat p) = n\,\mathrm{KL}(\hat p \| q)
\ge 0$ (Gibbs; Theory/information-theory), verified against $500$
random challengers. The counts $(n_1..n_K)$ are sufficient.

\paragraph{Report card.} Each coordinate is a bernoulli MLE:
unbiased, $\mathrm{Var}(\hat p_j) = p_j(1-p_j)/n$ (verified to a few
percent over $1000$ replications), jointly asymptotically normal with
covariance $\frac1n(\operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^{\!\top})$.

\begin{remark}[Where this object runs the repo]
Every softmax model estimates a multinoulli whose parameter depends
on $x$ (Algorithms/classification/multiclass/softmax); every row of a
Markov transition matrix is one (Theory/markov-chains); every
language model's next-token head is one
(text-to-text/). Equation \eqref{eq:mle} is why ``count and
normalize'' keeps appearing as the closed form.
\end{remark}
