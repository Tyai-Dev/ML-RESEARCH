\noindent
Every model in this repository is ``a loss plus an optimizer.'' This page
is the loss catalogue: what each one is, where it comes from, what its
population minimizer \emph{remembers} about the data, and how it treats
outliers. \texttt{losses\_demo.py} verifies the quantitative claims.

\section{The loss we mean, and the losses we use}

For classification the loss we \emph{care about} is the 0-1 loss
$\mathbf{1}[h(x) \ne y]$ --- the learnability note's object. We rarely
optimize it directly: it is discontinuous, non-convex, and ERM under it
is NP-hard for most classes. Instead we minimize a \emph{surrogate}: a
convex loss whose minimization is tractable and whose minimizer still
classifies correctly. The whole catalogue below is organized by this
divide --- the true loss, and the roofs we build over it.

\section{Margin losses for binary classification}

With $y \in \{-1,+1\}$ and a real-valued score $f(x)$, every loss below
depends only on the \emph{margin} $m = y\,f(x)$ (positive iff correct,
large iff confidently correct):
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{01} = \mathbf{1}[m \le 0], \quad
  \ell_{\mathrm{hinge}} = \max(0, 1-m), \quad
  \ell_{\mathrm{log}} = \log\bigl(1 + e^{-m}\bigr), \quad
  \ell_{\exp} = e^{-m},
  \label{eq:marginlosses}
\end{equation}
plus the squared hinge $\max(0,1-m)^2$ and the squared loss $(1-m)^2$.
Provenance: hinge is the SVM's slack (svm note); the logistic loss
\emph{is} the Bernoulli NLL in $\pm1$ convention ($\log(1+e^{-yf})
= -\log\sigma(yf)$ --- cross-entropy, the MLE notes); the exponential
loss is AdaBoost's; the perceptron loss $\max(0,-m)$ drives the
perceptron update. All the convex members dominate the 0-1 loss
pointwise ($\ell \ge \ell_{01}$, asserted on a grid in the script), so
small surrogate risk forces small classification risk:
$\Pr(\mathrm{error}) \le \mathbb{E}[\ell(m)]$.

\section{Calibration: what the minimizer remembers}

The deeper question about a surrogate: at a point $x$ with
$p = \Pr(Y{=}+1|x)$, what score does minimizing the \emph{population}
risk $p\,\ell(f) + (1-p)\,\ell(-f)$ produce?

\begin{proposition}[Population minimizers]
\begin{equation}
  f^\ast_{\mathrm{log}} = \log\frac{p}{1-p},
  \qquad
  f^\ast_{\mathrm{sq}} = 2p - 1,
  \qquad
  f^\ast_{\mathrm{hinge}} = \operatorname{sign}(2p - 1),
  \qquad
  f^\ast_{\exp} = \tfrac12\log\frac{p}{1-p}.
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
(Each by setting the derivative of $p\,\ell(f) + (1-p)\,\ell(-f)$ to
zero; the script confirms all four numerically.) Read the hierarchy:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Logistic}: $\sigma(f^\ast) = p$ --- the full conditional
    probability survives. Minimizing log-loss \emph{is} estimating
    $p(x)$; this is why logistic regression is calibrated and why
    cross-entropy owns probabilistic ML.
  \item \textbf{Squared}: $p$ is recoverable ($p = (f^\ast+1)/2$), but
    the loss keeps penalizing beyond $m > 1$ --- confidently correct
    points are punished, dragging boundaries (multi-output note's
    one-hot trick defect).
  \item \textbf{Hinge}: only $\operatorname{sign}(2p-1)$ survives ---
    the \emph{class}, not the probability. SVM scores are not
    calibrated; in exchange, the flat region ($\ell = 0$ for $m \ge 1$)
    ignores confident points entirely, producing the support-vector
    sparsity.
\end{itemize}
All of them are \emph{classification-calibrated}
($\operatorname{sign} f^\ast = \operatorname{sign}(p - \tfrac12)$, the
Bayes rule): minimizing any of these surrogates over rich enough
classes drives the 0-1 risk to the Bayes risk (Bartlett--Jordan--
McAuliffe). You choose among them by what you need to
\emph{keep} --- probabilities, sparsity, or simplicity --- not by
correctness.

\section{Regression losses: location and robustness}

For residual $r = y - f(x)$:
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{sq}} = \tfrac12 r^2,
  \qquad
  \ell_{\mathrm{abs}} = |r|,
  \qquad
  \ell_{\mathrm{Huber},\delta} =
  \begin{cases}
    \tfrac12 r^2 & |r| \le \delta\\[2pt]
    \delta\bigl(|r| - \tfrac{\delta}{2}\bigr) & |r| > \delta ,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:reglosses}
\end{equation}
plus the $\varepsilon$-insensitive $\max(0, |r|-\varepsilon)$ of SVR.
Their population minimizers are the classical location statistics:
squared $\to$ conditional \emph{mean} (the Gaussian MLE note), absolute
$\to$ conditional \emph{median}, Huber $\to$ in between. That single
fact is the robustness story: a gross outlier contributes $O(r^2)$ to
squared loss but only $O(|r|)$ to absolute and Huber --- the script fits
a constant to data with $10\%$ gross outliers, and the squared fit is
dragged to $9.5$ while absolute and Huber stay at the bulk's $5.0$.
Huber keeps the squared loss's smoothness and statistical efficiency
near zero while capping the influence of the tails --- the standard
compromise.

\section{Steepness is a personality trait}

For $m < 0$ (mistakes), compare growth: hinge is linear, logistic is
asymptotically linear, exponential is \emph{exponential}. The script
prints the penalty a badly mislabeled point pays at score $|f| = 10$:
hinge $11$, exponential $e^{10} \approx 22000$ --- AdaBoost's
exquisite outlier sensitivity in one number. Flat-side behavior matters
too: hinge is exactly $0$ for $m \ge 1$ (satisfied points are ignored:
sparsity, PA's ``passive''), while logistic never reaches zero (every
point keeps voting: no support-vector structure, but calibrated
probabilities). Steep left side = aggressive on errors; flat right side
= indifferent to safe points; every loss in \eqref{eq:marginlosses} is a
different setting of those two dials.

\section{Choosing, in one table}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
loss & convex & minimizer remembers & outlier response & used by\\
\midrule
0-1 & no & the class & indifferent & the target itself\\
hinge & yes & the class & linear & SVM, PA\\
squared hinge & yes & class (+ margin) & quadratic & smooth SVMs\\
logistic / CE & yes & the probability & $\sim$linear & GLMs, deep nets\\
exponential & yes & $\tfrac12$ log-odds & exponential & AdaBoost\\
squared (cls.) & yes & $2p-1$ & quadratic (both sides!) & one-hot LS\\
\midrule
squared (reg.) & yes & mean & quadratic & OLS, Gaussian MLE\\
absolute & yes & median & linear & robust regression\\
Huber & yes & mean$\leftrightarrow$median & quad.\ then linear & robust default\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
One sentence to keep: \emph{a loss function is a statement about which
mistakes you fear and what information you want the minimizer to
retain} --- probabilities (logistic), the decision alone (hinge), or a
robust location (absolute/Huber); everything else in this repository is
the machinery for descending it.
