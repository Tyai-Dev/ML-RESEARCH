\begin{abstract}
The promote/demote multiclass perceptron, and the theorem that makes
it a special case of the binary one: Kesler's construction embeds the
$K \times d$ weight matrix as a single vector acting on joint
features, under which the multiclass update \emph{is} the binary
update. Novikoff's mistake bound transfers verbatim. Both the
identity and the bound are verified by
\texttt{multiclass\_perceptron.py}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The algorithm}

Weights $W \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$; prediction
$\hat y(x) = \arg\max_k \langle W_k, x\rangle$. Online, upon a
mistake ($\hat y \ne y$):
\begin{equation}
  W_{y} \leftarrow W_{y} + x, \qquad
  W_{\hat y} \leftarrow W_{\hat y} - x
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
--- promote the true class's row, demote the offending one; correct
predictions change nothing (mistake-driven, distribution-free: no
model of $p(x)$ anywhere).

\section{The Kesler construction}

Define joint features $\Phi(x, k) \in \mathbb{R}^{Kd}$ by placing $x$
in the $k$-th block and zeros elsewhere, and let
$w \in \mathbb{R}^{Kd}$ be $W$ read row-wise.

\begin{theorem}[Multiclass is binary]
\label{thm:kesler}
For all $x, k$: $\langle W_k, x\rangle = \langle w, \Phi(x,k)\rangle$,
and the update \eqref{eq:update} equals the binary perceptron update
\begin{equation}
  w \;\leftarrow\; w + \Phi(x, y) - \Phi(x, \hat y).
  \label{eq:kesler}
\end{equation}
Consequently the two algorithms, run on the same example sequence,
produce identical mistake sequences and identical weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The score identity is immediate from the block structure. For the
update: $\Phi(x,y) - \Phi(x,\hat y)$ has $+x$ in block $y$, $-x$ in
block $\hat y$, zeros elsewhere --- exactly \eqref{eq:update} read
row-wise. Identical scores and identical updates imply identical
trajectories by induction over the sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Novikoff, lifted]
If some $W^*$ separates the data with joint-feature margin
$\gamma = \min_i \min_{k \ne y_i}
\langle w^*, \Phi(x_i,y_i) - \Phi(x_i,k)\rangle / \|w^*\|$ and
$R^2 = \max_i \|\Phi(x_i,y_i) - \Phi(x_i,k)\|^2 = 2\max_i\|x_i\|^2$,
then the number of mistakes is at most $(R/\gamma)^2$
(Algorithms/classification/binary/perceptron for the binary proof,
which applies unchanged to \eqref{eq:kesler}).
\end{corollary}

\section{Numerical verification}

Three separated Gaussian classes in the plane, $700$ training points
(seed 7):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Claim & Reference & Measured\\
\midrule
Matrix vs.\ Kesler mistake sequences & Thm.~\ref{thm:kesler}
  & identical (3 mistakes)\\
Matrix vs.\ Kesler weights & Thm.~\ref{thm:kesler} & equal
  (allclose)\\
Mistakes $\le (R/\gamma)^2$ & Cor.~1 & $3 \le 748$\\
Held-out accuracy & --- & $1.000$ ($n = 200$)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}
The bound's slack ($3$ vs.\ $748$) is typical: Novikoff is
worst-case over all separable sequences. The construction, not the
bound, is the point --- every binary mistake-bound theorem lifts to
multiclass through $\Phi$, which is also how multiclass PA and the
Crammer--Singer SVM are obtained in the sibling folders.
\end{remark}
