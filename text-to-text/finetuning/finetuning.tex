\begin{abstract}
The ladder's GPT speaks Shakespeare. This note hands it Jane Austen
(\emph{Pride and Prejudice}, sanitized into the checkpoint's 65-char
alphabet) and separates, with three numbers on the same Austen
validation slice, the three things people conflate as ``transfer
learning'': what the old model already knows (zero-shot), what a short
low-lr adaptation buys (finetuning), and what the pretrained
initialization is worth against a random start at the identical budget
(the finetune--scratch gap). All three claims are asserted, not
narrated. \texttt{finetune\_practical\_pytorch.py}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: tokenizer lock-in, now binding}

The checkpoint is welded to its alphabet (the tokenization note's
warning): Austen must be expressed in the Shakespeare model's 65
characters. Sanitization maps curly quotes to straight, em-dashes to
hyphens, and drops what remains outside the vocabulary ---
$99.94\%$ of the $723{,}374$ characters survive. Split $90/10$;
evaluation is NLL/char on the held-out last tenth.

\section{The three numbers}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
condition & Austen val NLL & meaning\\
\midrule
uniform & $4.174$ & knows nothing\\
zero-shot (Shakespeare weights) & $1.7341$ & knows \emph{English}\\
from scratch, 1000 steps & $1.2393$ & knows only Austen\\
finetuned, 1000 steps @ lr $10^{-4}$ & $\mathbf{1.0978}$
  & knows English \emph{and} Austen\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Zero-shot: knowledge transfers.} Untouched, the
Shakespeare model scores $1.73$ on Austen --- $2.4$ nats better than
uniform. Most of what a character LM knows (spelling, spacing,
punctuation, function words) is the \emph{language}, not the author.
That surviving knowledge is what finetuning steers.

\paragraph{Finetuning: steer, don't blast.} 1000 steps at learning
rate $10^{-4}$ --- $6\times$ below pretraining's peak, because a hot
optimizer would blast away the transferred knowledge before the new
gradient signal can redirect it (catastrophic forgetting's simplest
face). The model crosses below its zero-shot score within 100 steps
and settles at $1.0978$.

\paragraph{The gap: what pretraining is worth.} A fresh random GPT
given the \emph{identical} 1000-step budget (with the full rung-4
recipe, which is optimal for a cold start) reaches only $1.2393$. The
pretrained initialization is worth $0.1415$ nats/char at this budget
--- the finetuned run starts from ``knows English, wrong author,''
the scratch run from noise, and the budget is too short to relearn
English from scratch.

\begin{remark}[Austen is easier than Shakespeare]
The finetuned Austen NLL ($1.10$) is far below the model's own
Shakespeare score ($1.47$). That is a statement about the
\emph{corpora}, not the model: 19th-century narrative prose is more
predictable per character than dramatic verse with speaker turns,
archaic inflection, and meter. NLL measures the text's conditional
entropy as much as the model's skill --- the measuring stick from
rung 1, cutting in a new direction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sample]
The finetuned model's samples switch register completely --- from
blank-verse dialogue to drawing-room prose, complete with the novel's
protagonist: ``\ldots\texttt{there she and the restraint opy of the
conduct of these strongings were to be bringing Elizabeth's.}''
Grammar is still 3.2M-params rough; the \emph{style} transferred in a
thousand steps.
\end{remark}

The animation plays the race: finetune and scratch curves descending
under the zero-shot line, while the finetuned samples morph from
Shakespeare to Austen.

\begin{remark}[Scope]
This is full-parameter finetuning at toy scale. The industrial
variants --- freezing layers, LoRA (rank-constrained updates),
instruction tuning, RLHF --- are refinements of exactly this
experiment's logic and belong to future notes.
\end{remark}
