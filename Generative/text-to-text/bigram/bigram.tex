\begin{abstract}
The first language model in this repository, and the closing of a loop:
a character bigram model is a \emph{conditional multinoulli MLE}, so
everything proved in the classic-ML track --- counts as closed forms,
cross-entropy as the loss, softmax gradients, SGD noise balls, autograd
verification --- applies verbatim to text. Three companion scripts share
one corpus (Tiny Shakespeare, char-level, \texttt{Generative/text-to-text/common.py}):
\texttt{bigram\_theoretical.py} builds the model by counting and proves
no challenger beats it; \texttt{bigram\_practical\_pure.py} reaches the
\emph{same} table by gradient descent and SGD with hand-derived
gradients, animated; \texttt{bigram\_practical\_pytorch.py} verifies
autograd against the hand gradient step-for-step, then trains
full-batch on the GPU. This document also fixes the ladder's measuring
stick: validation NLL per character, equivalently perplexity.
\end{abstract}

\section{The model: text as a Markov chain}

Fix a character vocabulary of size $V$ (here $V = 65$). The bigram
model assumes each character depends only on its predecessor:
\begin{equation}
  P(c_1, \dots, c_T)
  \;=\; P(c_1) \prod_{i=1}^{T-1} P(c_{i+1} \mid c_i).
  \label{eq:markov}
\end{equation}
The parameters are a $V \times V$ table $p(b \mid a)$ --- one
conditional multinoulli per current-character $a$. Let
$n_{ab}$ count how often $a$ is followed by $b$ in the training text,
$n_a = \sum_b n_{ab}$, and $n = \sum_a n_a$ (the number of transition
pairs). Grouping identical factors of the likelihood --- the bernoulli
move, one level up ---
\begin{equation}
  L(p) \;=\; \prod_{a}\prod_{b}\; p(b \mid a)^{\,n_{ab}},
  \qquad
  \ell(p) \;=\; \sum_{a}\sum_{b} n_{ab}\,\log p(b \mid a).
  \label{eq:loglik}
\end{equation}
No term of $\ell$ couples two rows: the problem separates into $V$
independent multinoulli MLE problems.

\section{The theoretical solution: counting}

\begin{proposition}
For every row $a$ with $n_a > 0$, the maximizer of \eqref{eq:loglik}
subject to $\sum_b p(b \mid a) = 1$ is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\hat{p}(b \mid a) \;=\; \frac{n_{ab}}{n_a}\;}
  \label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
--- count and normalize; $m/n$, one level up.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Row $a$'s Lagrangian is
$\sum_b n_{ab} \log p(b\mid a) - \lambda\bigl(\sum_b p(b \mid a)-1\bigr)$.
Stationarity gives $n_{ab}/p(b \mid a) = \lambda$, so
$p(b \mid a) = n_{ab}/\lambda$; the constraint forces
$\lambda = \sum_b n_{ab} = n_a$. The row objective is strictly concave
where it matters ($-n_{ab}/p^2 < 0$), so the point is the unique
maximum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gibbs: optimality, information-theoretically]
For any challenger row distribution $q$, the excess NLL is exactly a
KL divergence:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{NLL}_a(q) - \mathrm{NLL}_a(\hat{p})
  \;=\; n_a\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\hat{p}_a \,\|\, q\bigr) \;\ge\; 0,
  \label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
with equality iff $q = \hat{p}_a$ (Theory/information-theory). The
theoretical script verifies this against $500$ random challengers.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sufficiency]
The likelihood \eqref{eq:loglik} touches the data only through the
count matrix $(n_{ab})$: those $V^2$ numbers are a sufficient statistic
for the $\sim$1M-character corpus. Section~\ref{sec:autograd} will
exploit this computationally.
\end{remark}

\section{The measuring stick: perplexity}

Every model on the ladder is judged by its average NLL per character
(in nats) on the \emph{same} held-out slice, or equivalently its
exponential:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\mathrm{PPL} \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac1T
  \textstyle\sum_i \log p(c_{i+1}\mid c_i)\Bigr)\;}
  \label{eq:ppl}
\end{equation}
--- ``the effective number of choices per character.'' A uniform
guesser scores $\exp(\log V) = 65$; the bigram count model scores
$11.61$ on train.

\begin{remark}[The zero-count catastrophe --- why the ladder continues]
$187$ of the $111{,}539$ validation transitions never occur in
training; the pure MLE assigns them $\hat p = 0$ and the validation NLL
is \emph{infinite}. Laplace smoothing
$(n_{ab}+\alpha)/(n_a+\alpha V)$ with $\alpha = 1$ patches this
($\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{val}} = 2.4819$, $\mathrm{PPL} = 11.96$), but
the lesson stands: \textbf{counting cannot generalize to unseen
contexts}, and with context length $k$ the table needs $V^k$ rows.
Rung~2 (embeddings + MLP) exists because of this paragraph.
\end{remark}

\section{The practical solution: descend the same objective}

The ML translation: the average training NLL, written with counts and
row weights $w_a = n_a/n$, is a count-weighted cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  \;=\; -\frac1n \sum_{a,b} n_{ab} \log p(b \mid a)
  \;=\; \sum_a w_a\, \mathrm{CE}\bigl(\hat{p}_a,\, p_a\bigr).
  \label{eq:celoss}
\end{equation}
Parameters: a $V \times V$ table of \emph{logits} $W$, with
$p_a = \smax(W_a)$ --- the same coordinate-change move as bernoulli's
sigmoid, one level up: softmax maps each unconstrained row onto the
simplex, so the constrained problem becomes free optimization. The
gradient (derived in softmax-regression, reused here): for row $a$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_a}
  \;=\; w_a\bigl(\smax(W_a) - \hat{p}_a\bigr),
  \qquad
  g_{(a,b)}(W) \;=\; \smax(W_a) - e_b \;\text{ on row } a\;}
  \label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}
(full and per-sample forms; $e_b$ the one-hot) --- prediction minus
target, the GLM residual signature yet again.

\paragraph{Route GD.} The loss separates across rows, so we descend
each row with its own full gradient $\smax(W_a) - \hat p_a$: $V$
independent convex problems solved simultaneously, whose unique fixed
point is $\smax(W_a) = \hat{p}_a$. \emph{Gradient descent converges to
the count table}: measured, $\max_{ab}|\smax(W)-\hat p| =
2.9\times10^{-4}$ after $3000$ steps ($\eta = 2$). Two routes, one
destination --- now on language.

\paragraph{Route SGD.} Visit transition pairs one at a time and apply
the per-sample gradient of \eqref{eq:grad} --- unbiased, cheap, noisy,
and it never builds the count table at all. Constant step size
($\eta = 0.5$, one epoch $= 10^6$ steps) leaves the last iterate in a
noise ball at NLL $2.5665$; Polyak--Ruppert averaging over the second
half of training collapses it to $2.4551$, three thousandths of a nat
above the floor $2.4519$. Bernoulli's noise-ball lesson, verbatim, at
$65 \times 65$ scale.

\section{Autograd, and sufficiency as a computational trick}
\label{sec:autograd}

\texttt{F.cross\_entropy}$(W_a, b)$ is exactly the per-sample NLL
through the softmax coordinate change, so autograd must produce
\eqref{eq:grad}. \textbf{Proof by computation:} driving torch's SGD
through the identical pair schedule, step size, and start $W = 0$ (in
\texttt{float64}) as the hand-written SGD, and recording the probe
$P(\texttt{h} \mid \texttt{t})$ at every step, the two trajectories
agree to $4.4\times10^{-16}$ --- one floating-point ulp --- over
$20{,}000$ steps.

Sufficiency then pays a computational dividend: by \eqref{eq:celoss}
the \emph{full-batch} loss over $10^6$ pairs is computable from the
$65\times65$ count matrix alone, so ``full-batch training'' is a dense
$V \times V$ operation. Adam on the RTX 4070 minimizes it in $2.8$
seconds and lands $2.2\times10^{-4}$ from the count table --- the third
implementation to arrive at the same destination.

\section{Experiment summary}

Tiny Shakespeare, char-level, $V = 65$; train = first 90\%
($1{,}003{,}853$ transitions), val = last 10\%, seed 7.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
route & train NLL & note \\
\hline
uniform baseline & $\log 65 = 4.174$ & PPL 65 \\
count MLE \eqref{eq:counts} & $2.4519$ & the floor; PPL $11.61$ \\
GD, $3000$ steps & $2.4519$ & $\max$ prob.\ error $2.9\times10^{-4}$ \\
SGD, last iterate & $2.5665$ & the noise ball \\
SGD, Polyak & $2.4551$ & averaging cancels the noise \\
full-batch Adam (GPU) & $2.4519$ & from counts alone, $2.8$s \\
\hline
val (Laplace $\alpha{=}1$) & $2.4819$ & \textbf{PPL 11.96 — rung-1
  number to beat} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The animations: the pure script plays GD learning the language ---
the error heatmap $|\smax(W_k) - \hat p|$ fading to black, the NLL
falling onto the count-MLE floor, and the model's text samples
sharpening from uniform noise toward Shakespeare-shaped babble, in
sync. The pytorch script draws the hand and autograd probe trajectories
simultaneously --- they never separate --- beside their per-step gap
pinned at $10^{-16}$.

One sentence to keep: \emph{a language model is a conditional
distribution estimated by maximum likelihood; the bigram just makes
that fact small enough to prove.}
