\begin{abstract}
Rung 1 proved that with one character of context, counting is
unbeatable. This rung measures what happens when you ask counting for
more context --- it dies, quantitatively, by $k=4$ --- and replaces it
with the first \emph{parametric} language model: characters embedded in
$\mathbb{R}^d$, contexts mapped to logits by a one-hidden-layer
network. Three scripts: \texttt{mlp\_theoretical.py} measures the
curse of dimensionality (the U-turn: more context, worse model);
\texttt{mlp\_practical\_pure.py} implements \textbf{backpropagation by
hand} and proves it identical to autograd through every parameter
tensor; \texttt{mlp\_practical\_pytorch.py} trains the model on the GPU
and beats every count model, while animating the embedding space
organizing itself.
\end{abstract}

\section{Counting with more context, and its death}

The $k$-gram count model conditions on the previous $k$ characters:
rung 1's Lagrange argument applies verbatim with one multinoulli row
per \emph{context} $\mathbf{c} \in \{1..V\}^k$, giving the MLE
$\hat p(b \mid \mathbf{c}) = n_{\mathbf{c}b}/n_{\mathbf{c}}$. The
mathematics is fine; the \emph{statistics} is not, because the table
has $V^k$ rows and the corpus has $\sim$$10^6$ positions. Measured on
Tiny Shakespeare (Laplace $\alpha = 1$, unseen contexts scored uniform):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrr}
\toprule
$k$ & possible contexts & seen & val ctx unseen & val NLL (PPL)\\
\midrule
1 & 65 & 65 & 0\% & 2.4819 \ (11.96)\\
2 & 4{,}225 & 1{,}380 & 0.17\% & 2.0684 \ (7.91)\\
3 & 274{,}625 & 11{,}228 & 1.39\% & \textbf{1.9526 \ (7.05)}\\
4 & $1.8\times10^{7}$ & 48{,}539 & 4.20\% & 2.1737 \ (8.79)\\
5 & $1.2\times10^{9}$ & 133{,}293 & 10.12\% & 2.5931 \ (13.37)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The U-turn at $k=3$ is the curse of dimensionality made concrete: past
it, \emph{more information makes the model worse}, for two reasons the
script measures separately. Unseen contexts (10\% of validation at
$k=5$) force uniform guesses worth $\log 65 = 4.17$ nats each; and even
seen contexts have thin rows --- $n_{\mathbf{c}} \sim 1$ puts the
estimator report card's $\mathrm{Var} = p(1-p)/n$ at its worst. By
$k=5$ the model is \emph{worse than the bigram}.

\section{The parametric fix: distributed representations}

Stop giving every context its own row. Two moves (Bengio et al.\ 2003):
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{E = C[X]}_{\text{embed}}, \quad
  F = \operatorname{flat}(E), \quad
  \underbrace{H = \tanh(F W_1 + b_1), \quad
  Z = H W_2 + b_2}_{\text{map to logits}}, \quad
  \mathcal{L} = \mathrm{CE}(Z, Y),
  \label{eq:mlp}
\end{equation}
where $C \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$ embeds each character, a context
of $k$ characters becomes the concatenation of its embeddings
($F \in \mathbb{R}^{kd}$), and a one-hidden-layer network produces the
$V$ logits. Parameter count:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;Vd + kd\,h + h + hV + V
  \;=\; 67{,}673 \ \text{ at } k{=}8,\, d{=}24,\, h{=}256
  \quad\text{vs}\quad V^k(V{-}1) \approx 2\times10^{16}\;}
  \label{eq:params}
\end{equation}
--- \emph{linear} in $k$ instead of exponential. The payoff is
statistical, not just spatial: similar contexts share parameters
through $C$, so a context never seen in training still gets a sensible
prediction, because its \emph{characters} were seen. This is the
distributed representation --- the single idea that scales to GPT.

\section{Backpropagation, by hand}

To earn the right to use autograd on models too deep to check row by
row, we differentiate \eqref{eq:mlp} completely by hand. With batch
size $B$ and mean CE loss, the chain rule gives, layer by layer:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \begin{aligned}
  dZ &= \tfrac1B\bigl(\smax(Z) - \mathrm{onehot}(Y)\bigr)
      &&\text{(the GLM residual, again)}\\
  dW_2 &= H^{\!\top} dZ, \quad db_2 = \textstyle\sum_i dZ_i,
      \quad dH = dZ\, W_2^{\!\top}\\
  d\mathrm{pre} &= dH \odot (1 - H^2)
      &&(\tanh' = 1 - \tanh^2)\\
  dW_1 &= F^{\!\top} d\mathrm{pre}, \quad db_1 = \textstyle\sum_i
      d\mathrm{pre}_i, \quad dF = d\mathrm{pre}\, W_1^{\!\top}\\
  dC &= \operatorname{scatter\!-\!add}\bigl(X,\,
      \operatorname{unflat}(dF)\bigr)
      &&\text{(embedding rows are reused)}
  \end{aligned}\;}
  \label{eq:backprop}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Proof by computation.} Identical initialization, identical
batch schedule, identical SGD steps, \texttt{float64}: over $300$
steps, the numpy implementation of \eqref{eq:backprop} and torch
autograd agree to $1.8\times10^{-15}$ in the loss and to at most
$2.2\times10^{-16}$ in \emph{every parameter tensor} ($C, W_1, b_1,
W_2, b_2$). The chain rule, mechanized, is still our chain rule ---
now one hidden layer deep. From here on, autograd does the bookkeeping.

\section{Experiment: the rung is climbed}

Adam (lr $10^{-3}$ for 8k steps, $10^{-4}$ for 4k), batch 256, on the
RTX 4070: $12{,}000$ steps in $16.5$ seconds. Validation NLL:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{val}} = 1.7583 \ \text{nats/char},
  \qquad \mathrm{PPL} = 5.80\;}
  \label{eq:result}
\end{equation}
beating the bigram ($11.96$) and counting's sweet spot ($7.05$) --- the
first model on the ladder that generalizes to contexts it has never
seen. Samples now contain English words and phrase rhythm
(``\texttt{Upon thilal; be that in me before}\,\ldots'', ``\texttt{my
lord}'', ``\texttt{To speak}'') where the bigram produced only
letter-texture.

The animation is this rung's insight made visible: projecting every
training checkpoint of $C$ onto a fixed PCA plane, the 65 characters
drift from a random cloud into structure --- vowels together,
punctuation apart, capitals clustered --- \emph{with nobody telling the
model what a vowel is}. The loss carved that geometry. Alongside: the
train/val loss falling through both count-model baselines, and the
samples sharpening.

\begin{remark}[What still fails --- rung 3's opening]
The context is a \emph{fixed} window, position-rigid: parameters for
``3 characters back'' are separate from ``4 characters back,'' and
$k$ cannot grow without retraining. What we want is a model that
\emph{decides for itself} which previous characters matter ---
attention.
\end{remark}
