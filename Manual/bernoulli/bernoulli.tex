\section{Setup}

Let $x_1,\dots,x_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ with
$x_i \in \{0,1\}$. The likelihood and average negative log-likelihood (NLL)
are
\begin{equation}
  L(p) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p^{x_i}(1-p)^{1-x_i},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}(p) = -\bar{x}\log p - (1-\bar{x})\log(1-p),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{x} = \tfrac1n\sum_i x_i$. Note the data enters only through
$\bar{x}$ --- a sufficient statistic.

\section{The theoretical solution}

Setting $\mathcal{L}'(p) = -\bar{x}/p + (1-\bar{x})/(1-p) = 0$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat{p}_{\mathrm{MLE}} = \bar{x}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{L}''(p) = \bar{x}/p^2 + (1-\bar{x})/(1-p)^2 > 0$, the NLL is
strictly convex on $(0,1)$ and $\bar{x}$ is its unique minimizer.

\section{SGD on the NLL}

Parameterize $p = \sigma(t) = (1+e^{-t})^{-1}$ so the search is
unconstrained (by invariance of the MLE, optimizing $t$ and mapping back
gives the same estimate). The per-batch gradient is remarkably clean:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,
  \Bigl[-\tfrac1{|B|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in B} \log
  \mathrm{Bern}(x_i;\sigma(t))\Bigr]
  \;=\; \sigma(t) - \bar{x}_B ,
\end{equation}
so the update $t \leftarrow t - \eta\,(\sigma(t) - \bar{x}_B)$ pushes
$\sigma(t)$ toward each batch mean; averaged over batches the fixed point is
$\sigma(t^\ast) = \bar{x}$. Convexity in $t$ (the loss is
$\log(1+e^{t}) - \bar{x}_B\,t$ per batch) means SGD has one basin to find.

\section{What the experiment shows}

\texttt{mle.py} draws $n = 5000$ samples at $p = 0.3$ and reports both
estimates. One subtlety: with a constant step size, a single SGD iterate
never converges --- it wanders in a noise ball around the optimum, so the
script averages the final epoch's iterates (Polyak--Ruppert averaging),
after which the two estimates agree to a few $10^{-3}$. Two routes, one
optimum --- the likelihood surface, not the algorithm, decides where
estimation lands.
